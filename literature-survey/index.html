Literature survey at IIT Kanpur

Foundation models in financial signal processing

Notes from my survey of the research on foundation models applied to financial time series. For each paper, the question I keep asking is whether it actually holds up, not just whether it is new.

Note 1 October 2026

Pretrained forecasting models on stock returns: a cheaper baseline, not an edge

  • 6pretrained models, used out of the box
  • 5large US stocks, daily returns
  • 8 of 10tasks won by pretrained models
  • 2cases that beat a zero forecast with statistical significance

One of the most exciting parts of being at IIT Kanpur is the research culture around us. We are constantly exposed to high impact work in our domain and encouraged to engage with it critically, rather than just read it. You quickly learn to ask, “Does this actually hold up?” rather than just, “Is this new?”

That question has shaped how I’m approaching financial signal processing, especially the wave of foundation models now being applied to financial time series. One paper from our literature survey has stayed with me.

The study

Dr Miquel Noguer i Alonso and Rodolfo Pereira Franklin took six pretrained forecasting models, including Google’s TimesFM, Salesforce’s Moirai and Amazon’s Chronos. They used them straight out of the box on daily returns for Apple, Amazon, Google, JPMorgan and Meta, and compared them with five neural networks trained from scratch on each stock.

The pretrained models won 8 of the 10 tasks. But on Apple and JPMorgan, the winning model still did worse than simply forecasting a return of zero. Only two of the cases they tested beat that zero baseline in a statistically meaningful way.

Why the pretrained models win

What stood out to me was the authors’ explanation of why the pretrained models win at all. It isn’t necessarily because they understand markets. When all you have is one stock’s price history, a model that arrives with sensible assumptions makes fewer mistakes than one learning everything from scratch. Meta showed this advantage isn’t universal: there, a model trained only on Meta’s own history beat every foundation model.

My read

Generic pretraining gives you a cheaper baseline, not an edge.

Past returns carry very little information about future ones, and a better model can’t create signal that isn’t there. The more promising direction may be foundation models pretrained on financial data itself, which related work suggests closes much of the gap.
